How this was made

A real stadium, photographed from the air and from the ground, rebuilt in 3D, and put in your browser — with two real passages of play running on the field.

01

Capturing the stadium

A drone flew the venue and photographed it from every angle. ArcGIS Reality generated two different 3D products from that one set of overlapping imagery, and you can switch between them in the Captures panel.

An integrated mesh is a continuous meshed surface capturing the current state of an area. An automated process constructs 3D objects out of the overlapping imagery, and the result integrates the original image information as a textured mesh built on a triangular interlaced structure — representing built and natural features alike, walls and trees and ground, with elevation carried in the surface itself.

A Gaussian splat renders the scene from millions of splats — fuzzy ellipsoids, each defined by its position, rotation, size, colour and transparency — instead of the triangulated mesh above. They are reconstructed from the same 2D images using machine learning, and they hold fine detail that a mesh tends to lose: thin structures like railings, netting and cables. There is no solid surface underneath, which is why the two are worth having side by side.

02

Walking the ground

A drone looks down, so it never sees the front of a statue or what the plaza looks like at eye level. Those parts were captured on foot, with a phone, using Pix4D Catch, and dropped into the same 3D model in exactly the right spot — which is what makes the two surveys one place rather than two.

03

Putting it in a browser

The whole thing runs in a web page, built with the ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript. Nothing is installed and nothing is pre-rendered — the 3D is streamed and drawn live as you move.

That is also what the rest of the buttons are: measuring distances, areas and volumes on the real capture; dragging the time of day around to move the sun and the shadows; the floodlights coming on once it is actually dark; real current weather at the stadium; and the night sky with the Milky Way in the position it would genuinely be in.

04

The Live Action replays

Two passages of real sport, played out on the field. The field itself is painted on, because the pitch was covered when the stadium was photographed.

These are not animations somebody drew. Both come from real match tracking — the position of every one of the 22 players and the ball, recorded many times a second during an actual game. The runs, the speeds and the angles are the ones that happened. One is a 75-yard touchdown; the other is a goal worked from a tackle in midfield.

Both datasets are public. The gridiron play comes from the NFL's Big Data Bowl release, and the football from Metrica Sports' open sample data, which is anonymised at source.
